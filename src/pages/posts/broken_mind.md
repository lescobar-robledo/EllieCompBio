I feel like my mind is in pieces—small shards and fragments scattered across the room. They cut like broken glass, and the ground is bloodied by my footprints as I pace, searching for some semblance of meaning. From the walls, I hear voices arguing, trying to reach a consensus on how to piece me back together.

—To reinvent myself—

That’s the solace I cling to—the hope that, piece by piece, I can figure out who I even am. Out of the corner of my eye, a faint glare catches my attention. It’s my curiosity, taking the form of a glass vase, meant to pour my mind back into place. I am me, and yet I am not.

Living my entire life repressing what I truly desired gave rise to an alternate persona—one born to carry the pain that way of living inflicted on my soul. He was given a name, in honor of the forefathers who came before him. He was taught how to speak, how to walk, how to dress, how to act. He learned to listen. He learned to think. He learned to love. But he never truly learned to love himself.

When he was young, he was forced to carry the burden of his father’s absence. He tried to please others, afraid he would crumble if left alone. But his curiosity saved him—it led him on a journey through the valley of knowledge, where he gathered all he could. He built a fortress of logic and thought to keep the agonizing pain of existence at bay.

He didn’t have many social skills, but his brightness drew in some wonderful partners along the way. Right after his academic and professional life suffered its greatest upheaval, he met someone who could just barely see through the—by then—thick barrier he had built to hide me from the world. He started a relationship with her, and when the opportunity came to migrate to a country with what seemed like an endless fountain of knowledge, he invited her to come along and explore it with him.

He got married and came to the United States to pursue a PhD with his partner. He never believed in labels, but when the government asked for one, he adopted one. He didn’t know that I had already begun to wake up—slowly making my way out of the inner shell to face the real world. While this was happening, she had been walking alongside him on his academic path, but in doing so, she lost her own.

Then, in what could only be described as a supernova of emotion, the three of us met. All of us hurt. All of us tired. And yet, the raw joy of acknowledging my existence pushed me to the front. He made sure I could take my first steps—he held my hand, and slowly but surely, he let me go. I finally walked on my own. She was still there, watching him fade, helping me rise too. But she also needed to find her own way, and she began working on herself.

But now we are at a crossroads, and it turns out she and I want to walk different paths. I know he would have walked with her—but the place they were headed isn’t where I want to go. I don’t speak like he did. I don’t walk like he did. I don’t even dress like he did. I listen differently, think differently, and I love differently.

He had a name—one passed down through tradition. And I have a name too, one I chose for myself.

I want to be held. I want to be swept off my feet. I want to be flattered. I want to be desired.  
And that’s something she doesn’t have to give.

I am at a loss. I do love her. I want to walk with her. But I also want to walk with many others. Loving and being loved is something I can share endlessly.

But she is zealous with her love. And it seems that if I were to keep walking with her, not only would I take a path I don’t want to, I’d also have to walk it alone—with no one else by my side.

We could walk alone. But her path was built on top of the one he started—and if I walked away, I’d risk destroying it. She would be left without a place to stand.

I could never do that to her. Not after the love he and I both shared for her.

So that brings us here—where my shattered self is trying to figure out who I really am.

He and I are the same. We share a past. We share our knowledge. We share our passions. We even share the same parents, the same DNA.

But we are different. We see the world differently. We hold different opinions, different perspectives. I love myself. He did not.

He was given a name. I chose mine.

So where do I begin, and where does he end?

I am afraid—because I know the answer.

After thirty long years of repressing myself for the sake of pleasing others, I wouldn’t change my path for anyone.

I’m smart, though. I always try to find paths where the outcomes are maximized. He and I are both good at calculating these kinds of things.

But calculating doesn’t make it easy.

What if, in wanting everything, I end up with nothing?

What if the person who could hold me, desire me, love me… can’t accept that I still want her in my life?

What if just the possibility of me loving and sharing with others is already too much for them?

I finally know who I am.  
Now comes the hard part—learning to live while embracing it.

As a scientist, my curiosity has always led me into more questions than answers. I know what it’s like to live in the darkness of uncertainty.

Even when I can’t sleep, when I feel like I can’t breathe, I still try to machinate and calculate.

And I know this is only momentary. I will return to bed eventually—because piece by piece, I will pull my mind together.

Until it’s time to shatter again.
